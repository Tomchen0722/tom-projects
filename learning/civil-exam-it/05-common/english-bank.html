A. 字彙（1～40 題）

1
The government plans to ____ a new online system to simplify tax filing.
(A) implement　(B) imply　(C) impose　(D) improve
詳解
答案：(A) implement 實施、推行
中譯：政府計畫推行一套新的線上系統，以簡化報稅程序。

其他選項：(B) imply 暗示｜(C) impose 強加、課徵（impose a tax 課稅）｜(D) improve 改善
搭配：implement a policy / system / plan —— 公文英文極高頻。
2
Only citizens who meet certain criteria are ____ for the housing subsidy.
(A) capable　(B) eligible　(C) available　(D) reliable
詳解
答案：(B) eligible 有資格的
中譯：只有符合特定條件的民眾才具備申請住宅補貼的資格。

四個 -able 結尾字的區別：
eligible for/to V符合資格的（公文最常用）
capable of有能力做…的
available可取得的、有空的
reliable可靠的
記法：eligible 講的是資格，capable 講的是能力。
3
The data ____ exposed the personal information of over 100,000 residents.
(A) breach　(B) branch　(C) bridge　(D) brief
詳解
答案：(A) breach（資料）外洩、違反
中譯：這起資料外洩事件使十萬名以上居民的個人資料曝光。

資安必背字：data breach 資料外洩｜breach of contract 違約｜breach of duty 違反義務
其他選項：(B) branch 分支、分行｜(C) bridge 橋樑｜(D) brief 簡短的／簡報
4
Regular software updates help reduce system ____ to cyberattacks.
(A) durability　(B) vulnerability　(C) capability　(D) possibility
詳解
答案：(B) vulnerability 脆弱性、（資安）漏洞
中譯：定期更新軟體有助於降低系統面對網路攻擊時的脆弱性。

資安對照：vulnerability 漏洞｜threat 威脅｜risk 風險｜patch 修補程式
形容詞：vulnerable to + N（易受…傷害的）——常考搭配。
5
The committee will ____ the effectiveness of the program before extending it.
(A) evacuate　(B) evaluate　(C) elevate　(D) eliminate
詳解
答案：(B) evaluate 評估
中譯：委員會將先評估該計畫的成效，再決定是否延長。

四個易混字：(A) evacuate 疏散｜(C) elevate 提升｜(D) eliminate 消除
名詞形：evaluation 評估——與 assessment（評量）常互換。
6
Public agencies must ensure that their websites are ____ to people with disabilities.
(A) accessible　(B) acceptable　(C) accountable　(D) adaptable
詳解
答案：(A) accessible 可及的、無障礙的
中譯：公務機關必須確保其網站對身心障礙者而言是可及的（無障礙的）。

正是「無障礙網頁」的英文：web accessibility。
其他選項：(B) acceptable 可接受的｜(C) accountable 應負責的（accountable to the public 對公眾負責）｜(D) adaptable 適應力強的
7
The new regulation aims to ____ the administrative process and cut waiting time.
(A) streamline　(B) stream　(C) strengthen　(D) stretch
詳解
答案：(A) streamline 精簡（流程）
中譯：新法規旨在精簡行政流程並縮短等候時間。

行政英文高頻字：streamline the process / procedure
近義：simplify 簡化｜expedite 加速
8
Local governments received a ____ from the central government to build the facility.
(A) subsidy　(B) subsidiary　(C) substitute　(D) submission
詳解
答案：(A) subsidy 補助（款）
中譯：地方政府獲得中央政府的補助款以興建該設施。

其他選項：(B) subsidiary 子公司｜(C) substitute 替代品｜(D) submission 提交
相關：grant 補助金｜funding 經費｜allocate funds 撥款
9
All employees are required to ____ with the new security guidelines.
(A) comply　(B) complain　(C) complete　(D) compete
詳解
答案：(A) comply（with）遵守
中譯：所有員工均須遵守新的資安規範。

介系詞是考點：comply with（不是 comply to）
名詞形：compliance 法遵——資安與稽核文件的核心字。
10
The report ____ that air quality has improved over the past decade.
(A) indicates　(B) invites　(C) invents　(D) invests
詳解
答案：(A) indicates 顯示、指出
中譯：報告指出，過去十年間空氣品質已有改善。

「顯示」家族（閱讀測驗超高頻）：indicate、suggest、reveal、demonstrate、show
其中 suggest 在學術語境是「顯示」不是「建議」——這是常見的誤譯。
11
Taiwan is facing the challenges of an ____ society with a declining birth rate.
(A) aging　(B) aging up　(C) aged out　(D) age
詳解
答案：(A) aging 高齡化的
中譯：臺灣正面臨高齡化社會與出生率下降的挑戰。

必背詞組：an aging society 高齡社會｜declining birth rate 出生率下降｜life expectancy 平均餘命
這是國考英文最常出現的社會議題之一。
12
The policy has a ____ impact on small businesses in rural areas.
(A) significant　(B) signal　(C) signature　(D) signify
詳解
答案：(A) significant 顯著的、重大的
中譯：該政策對偏鄉小型企業有重大的影響。

詞性判斷即可解題：空格在 a ___ impact 之間，前有冠詞、後有名詞 → 必填形容詞。
(B) 名詞／動詞｜(C) 名詞｜(D) 動詞 → 全刪。

這題示範：不認識單字也能靠詞性拿分。
13
The agency will ____ a survey to understand residents' needs.
(A) conduct　(B) construct　(C) contact　(D) contract
詳解
答案：(A) conduct 執行、進行
中譯：該機關將進行一項調查，以了解居民的需求。

固定搭配：conduct a survey / study / research / investigation / audit
其他：(B) construct 建造｜(C) contact 聯絡｜(D) contract 契約／收縮
14
Digital transformation has ____ the way public services are delivered.
(A) transformed　(B) transferred　(C) transported　(D) translated
詳解
答案：(A) transformed 轉變、改造
中譯：數位轉型已徹底改變公共服務的提供方式。

trans- 家族：transform 轉變｜transfer 移轉｜transport 運輸｜translate 翻譯｜transmit 傳送
四個都是「跨越」，差別在後半段。
15
Officials must ____ the confidentiality of citizens' personal data.
(A) maintain　(B) contain　(C) obtain　(D) retain
詳解
答案：(A) maintain 維護、保持
中譯：公務人員必須維護民眾個人資料的機密性。

-tain 家族（極常混淆）：
maintain維持、維護
contain包含、抑制
obtain取得
retain保留
sustain維持、承受
相關名詞：confidentiality 機密性｜integrity 完整性｜availability 可用性——正是資安 CIA 三要素。
16
The proposal was rejected because it was not financially ____.
(A) feasible　(B) fashionable　(C) favorable　(D) familiar
詳解
答案：(A) feasible 可行的
中譯：該提案因財務上不可行而遭否決。

公文英文常見：feasibility study 可行性研究｜technically feasible 技術上可行
其他：(B) 時髦的｜(C) 有利的、贊成的｜(D) 熟悉的
17
Heavy rainfall has ____ in serious flooding in several districts.
(A) resulted　(B) caused　(C) led　(D) brought
詳解
答案：(A) resulted（in）導致
中譯：豪雨已導致數個行政區發生嚴重淹水。

介系詞是關鍵——空格後是 in：
result in導致（後接結果）
result from起因於（後接原因）
lead to導致（(C) 要配 to，不是 in）
cause / bring about直接接受詞，不用 in
result in / result from 的方向相反，是年年出現的考點。
18
The city has invested heavily in ____ such as roads, bridges, and public transit.
(A) infrastructure　(B) instrument　(C) institution　(D) instruction
詳解
答案：(A) infrastructure 基礎建設
中譯：該市已大量投資於道路、橋梁與大眾運輸等基礎建設。

解題線索：such as 後面舉的例子（roads, bridges, public transit）就是空格的定義。
「such as / for example / like」後面的例子，永遠是字彙題最好的線索。

延伸：critical infrastructure 關鍵基礎設施——資安法的核心概念。
19
Employees should be ____ of the risks of phishing emails.
(A) aware　(B) award　(C) awake　(D) away
詳解
答案：(A) aware（of）察覺、知悉
中譯：員工應了解釣魚郵件的風險。

固定搭配：be aware of + N｜raise awareness 提升意識
資安必背：security awareness training 資安認知訓練｜phishing email 釣魚郵件
20
The department is responsible for ____ resources to the most urgent projects.
(A) allocating　(B) alleviating　(C) allowing　(D) altering
詳解
答案：(A) allocating 分配
中譯：該部門負責將資源分配給最急迫的專案。

搭配：allocate resources / budget / funds to...
其他：(B) alleviate 緩解（alleviate poverty 減緩貧窮）｜(C) allow 允許｜(D) alter 改變
21
Despite the difficulties, the team managed to meet the ____.
(A) deadline　(B) headline　(C) outline　(D) guideline
詳解
答案：(A) deadline 期限
中譯：儘管困難重重，團隊仍設法趕上期限。

搭配：meet the deadline 趕上期限｜miss the deadline 錯過期限｜extend the deadline 展延
其他 -line：headline 標題｜outline 大綱｜guideline 準則
22
The government has taken steps to ____ the digital divide between urban and rural areas.
(A) bridge　(B) build　(C) break　(D) burn
詳解
答案：(A) bridge 弭平、跨越（作動詞）
中譯：政府已採取措施以弭平城鄉間的數位落差。

必背詞組：digital divide 數位落差｜bridge the gap / divide 弭平差距
同義說法：narrow the gap、close the gap。

注意：bridge 當動詞是「跨越、連接」的意思——名詞的印象反而會誤導。
23
Renewable energy sources are considered more ____ than fossil fuels.
(A) sustainable　(B) suitable　(C) sensible　(D) sociable
詳解
答案：(A) sustainable 永續的
中譯：再生能源被認為比化石燃料更具永續性。

環境類必背：renewable energy 再生能源｜fossil fuel 化石燃料｜sustainable development 永續發展｜carbon emissions 碳排放
24
The system automatically ____ unusual login attempts and alerts administrators.
(A) detects　(B) deletes　(C) delays　(D) delivers
詳解
答案：(A) detects 偵測
中譯：系統會自動偵測異常登入行為並通知管理者。

資安詞組：intrusion detection 入侵偵測｜detect anomalies 偵測異常｜alert 告警
解題線索：後半句 and alerts administrators（並通知管理者）→ 前面必然是「發現」了什麼。
25
The minister ____ the importance of protecting personal information.
(A) emphasized　(B) emptied　(C) employed　(D) enabled

中譯：部長強調保護個人資料的重要性。

「強調」家族：emphasize、stress、highlight、underscore——閱讀測驗的態度題常靠這些字判斷。
26
Applicants must submit the required documents ____ the end of this month.

詳解
答案：(A) no later than 不遲於
中譯：申請人須於本月底前繳交所需文件。

公文英文必背片語：
no later than不遲於（…之前）

27
The two agencies signed an agreement to ____ on cybersecurity issues.

詳解
答案：(A) collaborate 合作
中譯：兩機關簽署協議，就資安議題進行合作。

搭配：collaborate with someone on something
近義：cooperate 合作｜coordinate 協調｜partnership 夥伴關係
其他：(B) 崩塌｜(C) 蒐集｜(D) 碰撞
28
Statistics show a ____ increase in online fraud cases last year.
(A) sharp　(B) shallow　(C) shy　(D) short
詳解
答案：(A) sharp 急遽的
中譯：統計顯示，去年網路詐騙案件數量急遽增加。

描述數據變化的形容詞（閱讀測驗高頻）：
幅度大幅度小穩定
sharp、dramatic、significant、substantial、steepslight、marginal、modest、gradualsteady、stable、constant
動詞：rise / increase / soar / surge（升）｜fall / decline / drop / plunge（降）
29
The proposal must be approved by the board ____ it can be implemented.
(A) before　(B) after　(C) unless　(D) despite
詳解
答案：(A) before 在…之前
中譯：該提案須經董事會核准，才能付諸實施。

邏輯判斷：核准 → 實施（先核准，後實施）。
(B) after 顛倒因果｜(C) unless 除非（語意不合）｜(D) despite 是介系詞，後面不能接子句 → 詞性即可刪。

常考陷阱：despite / in spite of 是介系詞（+ N）；although / though 是連接詞（+ 子句）。
30
The new law will take ____ on January 1 next year.
(A) effect　(B) affect　(C) effort　(D) effective
詳解
答案：(A) effect（名詞）
中譯：新法將於明年 1 月 1 日生效。

固定片語：take effect 生效｜come into effect 開始施行｜in effect 實際上／有效

effect vs affect（每年必考）：
effect名詞：影響、效果（have an effect on）
affect動詞：影響（affect the outcome）
記法：a-ffect 是 action（動詞），e-ffect 是 end result（名詞）。

33
The findings ____ that early intervention leads to better outcomes.
(A) suggest　(B) suspect　(C) suppose　(D) supply
詳解
答案：(A) suggest（此處為「顯示、指出」）
中譯：研究發現顯示，及早介入可帶來較佳的成效。

⚠️ suggest 的兩個意思

閱讀測驗中，「The passage suggests...」是「文章指出／暗示」，不是「文章建議」。這是很常見的誤解。

39

B. 文法（41～70 題）

41時態
The department ____ the new system since last March.
(A) uses　(B) used　(C) has been using　(D) will use
詳解
答案：(C) has been using
中譯：該部門自去年三月起就一直使用這套新系統。

關鍵字：since → 表示「從過去某時間點持續到現在」→ 現在完成式或現在完成進行式。
「一直在使用」強調持續動作，故用完成進行式最佳。

時間副詞與時態對照（背這張表）：
since / for / so far / up to now現在完成式
yesterday / last / ago / in 2020過去式
by the time / by next year完成式
now / currently / at present現在（進行）式
42被動
The annual report ____ to the ministry last Friday.
(A) submitted　(B) was submitted　(C) has submitted　(D) submits
詳解

C. 克漏字（4 篇，71～90 題）

克漏字 1數位政府 · 71～75
Governments around the world are turning to digital technology to improve public services. In Taiwan, many applications that once required a personal visit to a government office can now be completed online (71) a few minutes. This shift has reduced waiting times and lowered administrative costs.

However, digitalization is not simply a matter of moving paper forms onto websites. If citizens still have to enter the same information three times on three different pages, the inconvenience has merely been (72) from the counter to the screen. 　　Public procurement accounts for a substantial share of government spending, and in recent years officials have begun to ask whether that spending could do more than simply obtain goods at the lowest price.

　　The traditional model rewards the cheapest compliant bid. Its appeal is obvious: price is easy to measure, easy to compare, and easy to defend if questioned. But the same qualities that make it defensible also make it blunt. A contractor who wins by cutting labour costs may deliver a technically acceptable product while paying below-market wages, and a supplier offering the lowest initial price may impose far higher maintenance costs over a decade.

　　Several countries have therefore introduced criteria beyond price — environmental impact, employment of disadvantaged workers, or the total cost of ownership across a product's lifetime. Evidence suggests these criteria can shift supplier behaviour, since firms adapt to whatever the buyer actually measures. The difficulty is that broader criteria are harder to evaluate objectively, and every additional dimension of judgment creates room for dispute, and occasionally for favouritism.

　　Experienced practitioners tend to converge on a middle position. Rather than abandoning price, they recommend defining criteria precisely enough that two independent evaluators would reach similar conclusions, publishing the weighting in advance, and documenting the reasoning behind each score. The aim is not to make procurement subjective, but to make it measure the right things — and to leave a record that can withstand scrutiny.

　　Every public notice makes an assumption about its reader. Most assume a reader who is literate, unhurried, and familiar with administrative vocabulary. In practice, the people who most need to understand a notice are often the least likely to match that description.

　　The plain language movement began from this observation. Its argument is not that complex ideas should be avoided, but that unnecessary complexity should be. A sentence such as "Applicants failing to submit the requisite documentation within the aforementioned period shall be deemed to have withdrawn their application" contains no difficult concept. It is merely written in a register that many readers must decode rather than simply read.

　　Rewriting it — "If we do not receive your documents by 30 June, we will treat your application as cancelled" — takes little effort and produces measurable results. Trials in several countries have found that plain-language versions of official letters increase correct responses and reduce the volume of clarifying phone calls, which in turn saves staff time. The savings are frequently larger than the cost of the rewriting.

　　Resistance nonetheless persists, and it is worth understanding why. Some officials associate simplified language with reduced legal precision, a concern that is occasionally justified and more often not. Others feel, without quite saying so, that formal language signals seriousness and authority. And a genuine difficulty exists where a notice serves two audiences at once: the citizen who must act on it, and the court that may later review it.

　　The most successful programmes have handled that tension by separating the functions rather than compromising between them — stating the essential message in plain terms at the top of the document, and placing precise legal wording in a clearly marked section below. The reader who needs to act reads the first part. The reader who needs the exact provision finds it in the second. Neither is served by a document that tries to be both at once, and succeeds at neither.

　　Most organizations describe training as an investment, and most measure it as an expense. The gap between those two statements explains a good deal about why training programmes so often disappoint.

　　Consider how the typical programme is evaluated. Attendance is recorded, a satisfaction form is distributed at the end of the session, and the results are reported as a percentage. Both measures are easy to collect and neither tells us whether anyone works differently afterwards. A course can be enjoyable, well attended, and entirely without effect.

　　Research on workplace learning consistently identifies a different factor as decisive: what happens in the weeks following the training. Skills that are not used within a short period are largely lost, and the strongest predictor of whether they are used is whether the participant's immediate supervisor expects them to be. A manager who asks, two weeks later, how the new method is working converts a course into a practice. A manager who never mentions it converts it into a memory.

　　This finding is inconvenient, because it locates responsibility somewhere other than the training department. It suggests that improving training may have less to do with better courses than with preparing supervisors — informing them beforehand what their staff will learn, and giving them a reason to follow up afterwards.

　　There is a further implication. If application depends on opportunity, then sending someone to a course they cannot use is worse than sending no one: it consumes budget, occupies time, and teaches participants that training is something to be endured rather than applied. Selecting the right participants is therefore not an administrative detail but a substantive decision — one that deserves as much attention as the choice of course itself.

📖 全文中譯 　　多數組織把訓練描述為一種投資，卻把它衡量成一筆費用。這兩句話之間的落差，相當程度上解釋了為什麼訓練方案往往令人失望。

　　看看典型的方案是怎麼被評估的：記錄出席率、課程結束時發一張滿意度問卷，然後把結果以百分比呈報。這兩項指標都很容易蒐集，卻沒有一項告訴我們：課後有沒有任何人的工作方式因此改變。一門課可以很有趣、出席率很高，而且完全沒有效果。

　　關於職場學習的研究，一致指出另一項因素才是決定性的：訓練結束後那幾週發生了什麼事。短期內沒有被使用的技能大多會流失，而能否被使用，最強的預測因子是：參與者的直屬主管是否期待他去使用。一位在兩週後開口詢問「那個新方法用起來如何」的主管，把一門課變成了一項做法；一位從未提起的主管，則把它變成了一段回憶。

　　這項發現令人不便，因為它把責任放在訓練單位以外的地方。它意味著：改善訓練，或許與「開更好的課」關係不大，反而與「事先準備好主管」關係較大——事前讓主管知道部屬將學到什麼，並給他一個事後追蹤的理由。

　　Discussions of data usually focus on quantity. Agencies describe how many records they hold, how many transactions they process, how many datasets they have released. Quantity is visible and easy to report. Quality is neither.

　　Yet quality determines whether the quantity is worth anything. A register in which ten per cent of addresses are out of date will misdirect one in ten notices, and no amount of additional records will correct that. Worse, errors of this kind are usually invisible from inside the system: the notice is sent, the record shows it was sent, and only the citizen who never received it knows otherwise.

　　Practitioners distinguish several dimensions of quality, of which accuracy is only one. Completeness asks whether required fields are populated; timeliness asks whether the record reflects the present rather than the past; consistency asks whether the same person is recorded identically across systems. A dataset can score well on one dimension and poorly on another, and improving one sometimes worsens another — collecting more fields, for instance, increases completeness while creating more opportunities for inconsistency.

　　What makes data quality genuinely difficult is that it is rarely anyone's job. The unit that collects a record often does not use it; the unit that uses it cannot correct it. Responsibility is therefore distributed so thinly that no one experiences the consequences of poor quality directly, and the problem persists not because it is technically hard but because it is organizationally unowned.

　　The remedy most often proposed is to assign a "data steward" for each significant dataset — a named person accountable for its accuracy and for resolving disputes about it. The role sounds bureaucratic, and sometimes is. But it addresses the actual cause, which is not ignorance of what good data looks like, but the absence of anyone whose responsibility it is to produce it.

　　When an agency must announce bad news — a service failure, a data breach, a policy that will disadvantage some group — its instinct is usually to say as little as possible. The reasoning seems sound: fewer words, fewer things to be criticized for.

　　Communication researchers have found the opposite pattern in the way audiences respond. Vagueness does not reduce criticism; it invites speculation, and speculation is almost always less favourable than the facts. An announcement that "a technical issue affected some users" prompts readers to imagine the worst plausible version of events, and to conclude that the organization is concealing something. A statement specifying what failed, how many were affected, and what is being done tends to produce a narrower and more accurate reaction, even when the facts themselves are unflattering.

　　Timing works similarly. Organizations frequently delay announcements until an investigation is complete, reasoning that it is better to speak once with full information. But affected people rarely wait; they compare notes, and a version of events assembled from fragments circulates in the meantime. By the time the official account appears, it is competing with an established narrative rather than establishing one.

　　None of this argues for announcing conclusions before they are known. The recommended practice is to communicate early about what is known, what is not yet known, and when the next update will come — and then to provide that update on schedule, even if it contains little new information. The reliability of the schedule turns out to matter nearly as much as the content.

　　What makes this difficult is not that the principle is unclear but that following it requires accepting short-term discomfort — admitting uncertainty publicly — in exchange for a longer-term benefit that is harder to observe. Organizations that manage this trade-off well are rarely praised for it, because the crises they avoid leave no trace.

　　Every rule is written with a typical case in mind. The difficulty is that rules are then applied to all cases, including those the drafter never imagined.

　　Two strategies exist for handling this. The first is to write rules in detail, anticipating as many situations as possible and specifying the treatment of each. The second is to write broad standards and rely on the judgment of the official applying them. Each has a characteristic failure. Detailed rules produce absurd outcomes when reality does not match the categories; broad standards produce inconsistency, because different officials reach different conclusions on similar facts.

　　Which failure is preferable depends on circumstances that are often left unexamined. Where cases are numerous and similar, and where consistency matters more than fit, detailed rules are usually better: the applicant benefits from predictability, and the official is protected from pressure to make exceptions. Where cases vary widely and the cost of a wrong outcome is high, standards allow the response to match the situation.

　　A third consideration is frequently decisive but rarely stated: the capability of those who will apply the rule. Standards demand judgment, and judgment requires training, experience, and time to deliberate. An organization that assigns broad discretion to inexperienced staff working under heavy caseloads has not chosen flexibility; it has chosen inconsistency while calling it flexibility.

　　The practical conclusion is that rule design and organizational capacity cannot be decided separately. A rule that would work well in one office may fail in another, not because the rule differs but because the conditions of its application do. This is uncomfortable for anyone who hopes to draft a rule once and apply it everywhere, which is to say for most rule-drafters — and it explains why so many well-written rules produce disappointing results in practice.
